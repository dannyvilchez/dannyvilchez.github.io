In this post-AI world, the internet feels more saturated with content than ever before.

People are posting at an overwhelming pace, and with this exponential increase in text and code, it seems like humanity has collectively decided that the answer is to simply do more. And with AI-powered tools, it’s never been easier to increase your output.

Like most things in life, this is both good and bad, or maybe neither good nor bad.

This post isn’t intended to put me on some moral high ground of, _“If you’re using AI to post more content, then you’re a bad person and I’m better because I don’t.”_ But it does feel like we’re prioritizing output above all else, and as far as the tech industry goes, I don’t think that will change. In my opinion, we will bury ourselves in AI-generated software and content simply because it costs less to produce.

So I find myself asking: what kind of creator and programmer do I want to be?

Do I want to be a purist? Someone who fuels their ego by judging others for using these tools, while being a hypocrite because I use them too, but claim there’s a “right way” to do it. Or do I want to be someone who fully offloads my coding and thinking to AI, inevitably losing my critical thinking skills and the joy that comes from overcoming cognitive obstacles?On top of that, how do I shield myself professionally from the impact this is having on knowledge work and the hiring process? It’s never been easier to be a charlatan. We can all play the wizard behind the LLM curtain and fool others into believing we’re smarter and more competent than we really are. And the more we offload to LLMs, the more it becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy.

In reality, I don’t know if optimizing for output will make me happy. I’ve found that my happiness comes from those private wins, those quiet moments of overcoming obstacles.

So this blog, and how I behave online moving forward, will not optimize for output.

I will optimize to increase the level of expertise I can achieve through my work.

It will mean reinventing the wheel. It will mean taking longer to finish and post projects. It will mean doing things that are inefficient. Because when someone stumbles across this blog, or my LinkedIn, or my other social pages, I don’t want them to see superficial, AI-generated work. I want them to see work that was carefully curated as an expression of who I am as a developer.

This doesn’t mean I won’t use LLMs.

It doesn’t mean I won’t include them in the products I deliver.

What it does mean is that I won’t use them to industrialize my work.
